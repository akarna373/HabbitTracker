Habbit Privacy Policy
Effective September 13, 2026

Your data stays on your phone.

Habbit helps you track, reduce, or quit habits like smoking, drinking, and pan masala use — and build good ones, like studying, fitness, reading, or mindfulness. This page explains exactly what the app does with your information — in plain language first, then in full.

In short
  • Everything you log stays in a local database on your device. There is no account, no server, and no cloud sync.
  • Location is only used, on your device, to notice when you're back at a spot tied to a habit you're quitting — and only if you turn that on.
  • We don't run ads, analytics, or trackers, and we never sell or share your data.
  • You can see, rename, or delete any recorded location, or delete a habit and its history, at any time.
1

Who this policy covers

Habbit is developed by an individual developer, Abhijeet. This policy applies to the Habbit Android app and describes how it handles information when you use it.

2

What's stored, and where

Every habit you create — its name, daily logs, amounts, spending, reflections, microtasks, and reminder times — is saved in a local SQLite database on your device only. Habbit has no user accounts, no backend server, and no cloud sync of any kind. Nothing you type into the app leaves your phone.

Uninstalling the app permanently deletes this database. Deleting a single habit from within the app deletes its history along with it.

3

Location: how it actually works

Location tracking is off by default and lives entirely per-habit, in two separate steps you turn on yourself:

  • Track smoking locations — when you enable this for a habit, tapping the "+" to log an entry also records your device's coordinates at that moment. Habbit compares these points against each other, on your device, to notice spots you visit repeatedly.
  • Warn me even when the app is closed — a second, separate toggle. Turning it on shows you an in-app explanation before Android ever asks for background location permission. Once granted, Habbit uses Android's geofencing system to notice when you arrive at one of your own recorded spots, even with the app fully closed, and shows a local notification.

In both cases, your location is compared only against your own past entries, only on your device. It is never transmitted anywhere, never sent to us or anyone else, and never used for advertising. You can open Manage recorded locations from any location-tracked habit at any time to see every spot Habbit has learned, rename it, or delete it — and you can turn either toggle off whenever you like.

4

Notifications

Reminders, nightly summaries, and location-deterrent alerts are all scheduled and shown directly by your phone's operating system. Habbit does not use any push-notification server — nothing about a notification is sent off your device to make it appear.

5

No analytics, ads, or third parties

Habbit contains no analytics software development kit, no advertising network, no crash-reporting service, and no other third-party code that could collect or transmit your data. We do not know how you use the app beyond what you can see yourself, because nothing is reported back to us.

6

Sharing and selling

We do not sell, rent, trade, or otherwise share your data with any third party, for any purpose. There is nothing to share — your data is stored only on your own device, and we have no access to it.

8

Children's privacy

Habbit is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from children.

9

Your choices

  • Turn location tracking, background detection, or reminders off per habit, at any time.
  • View, rename, or delete any recorded location from that habit's "Manage recorded locations" screen.
  • Delete a habit to remove its full history.
  • Uninstall the app to permanently remove all data Habbit has stored.
10

If this policy changes, the effective date at the top of this page will be updated, and any change that materially affects how your data is handled will also be noted in-app.
